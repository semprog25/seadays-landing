River cruise or ocean cruise

A river cruise and an ocean cruise both move you between places while you sleep. They do not feel like the same trip. On a river ship you are usually close to the town, you unpack once, and most days include a stop. On an ocean ship the vessel is the destination for long stretches, and a port day can mean a tender, a long pier, or a full day ashore.

Choose from the day you want, not from a list of famous ports. If you want to walk off into a city center and be back for dinner, a river itinerary is built for that. If you want sea days, bigger public rooms, and ports that can be hours from the dock, an ocean itinerary is built for that.

What changes day to day

  • River ships are smaller. Lines for meals and the gangway are shorter, and the cabin is often the quiet place.
  • Ocean ships have more to do when you are not in port. They also take longer to get on and off.
  • River port days are frequent. Ocean sailings mix port days with sea days, so the plan has to survive a day with no stop.
  • Included excursions are more common on river trips. On ocean trips you usually decide, port by port, what is worth leaving the ship for.

Neither style is cheaper in a way you can assume from the brochure. Compare what is included, how you get to the embarkation city, and whether you will pay for days ashore.

How to decide

Write down whether you care more about the towns or about time on the ship. If a tender, a long walk to the terminal, or a late all-aboard would ruin the day, say so before you book. If you want several nights of entertainment and are happy to skip a port, an ocean sailing fits that.
