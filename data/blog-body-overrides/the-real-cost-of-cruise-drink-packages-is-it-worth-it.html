What a drink package actually is

A cruise drink package is a prepaid beverage plan sold by the cruise line. Most mainstream packages cover a defined list of drinks up to a per-drink cap, plus gratuities in some fares and not in others. Soda-only, non-alcoholic, and beer-and-wine plans are separate products. This page is about deciding whether any package beats paying as you go—not about naming a current price, which changes by line, sailing, and promotion.

Always read the current terms for your booking. Packages can exclude bottled water brands, fresh juices, specialty coffee, room-service drinks, or drinks above a posted limit. Mini-bar items are often extra.

The only math that matters

A package is worth it when your expected prepaid-covered drinks would cost more à la carte than the package, after taxes and auto-gratuities.

Use this break-even sketch (plug in your sailing’s numbers):

  1. Package price per person, per day (or voyage total ÷ days).
  2. Typical covered drink price onboard, including the auto-gratuity if it is added per drink.
  3. Drinks per person per day you will actually order from the covered list—not the number you wish you would order.

If (drinks × à la carte price) is below the daily package price, paying as you go wins. If you routinely exceed that, the package can win. SeaDays has a dedicated walkthrough of that arithmetic in the SeaDays drink package calculator math and a “loses money” view in when the beverage package actually loses money.

Who it usually helps

  • Guests who order several covered alcoholic drinks every sea day and port evening.
  • Guests who would buy specialty coffee or mocktails all day on a soda/non-alcoholic plan that actually covers those items.
  • Sailings where à la carte prices are high and the package is on a genuine pre-cruise discount—not a “discount” off an inflated onboard rate.

Who it usually does not help

  • Light drinkers, or anyone who mostly drinks included water, iced tea, and dining-room coffee.
  • Guests who go ashore most days and drink on land (packages do not follow you into port).
  • Anyone who will not use the package on embarkation afternoon or disembarkation morning—those half-days still count as full days on many plans.
  • Cabins where the line requires every adult to buy the same alcoholic package. One heavy drinker cannot subsidize three light drinkers.

Hidden costs people miss

Auto-gratuities on packages can be a daily add-on rather than included. Some lines charge a daily gratuity even if you barely drink. Specialty dining pairings, bottled wine at dinner, and souvenir glasses are often outside the package. If you care about wine at dinner, compare a bottle’s onboard price to several package pours—not to a restaurant on land.

Age rules matter. Alcoholic packages are for guests who meet the line’s drinking age (21 on most ships calling on the US; 18 on some other itineraries). Do not assume a teen soda package equals an adult plan.

How to decide before you buy

  1. List what you drank on your last vacation, honestly.
  2. Open the line’s current package inclusions for your sailing dates.
  3. Run the break-even with the SeaDays calculator articles above, or the in-app drink package calculator.
  4. If you are within a drink or two of break-even, skip the package. Unused prepaid drinks are a worse outcome than one extra à la carte cocktail.
