What a 30-day cruise actually requires

Nobody at SeaDays lived on a ship for 30 days to write this. The old version of the page was written as a personal diary. That is removed. What follows is the booking and logistics checklist for a month afloat, which is either one long voyage or several cruises placed back to back.

One voyage or several bookings

A single 30-day itinerary is one ticket, one cabin, and the line’s rules for that voyage. Back-to-back cruises are separate bookings that happen to use the same cabin. Ask the line, in writing, whether you must leave the ship, collect luggage, or clear immigration between segments. Do not assume “same cabin number” means you can stay aboard. The answer is in that line’s back-to-back policy, not in a forum comment.

Papers, medicine, and money

The CDC’s cruise ship travel page tells you to carry prescriptions for the whole trip plus a delay, and to check whether your health insurance pays care abroad and on the ship. A month is long enough that a 10-day supply is the wrong pack. Visas and entry rules depend on the countries the itinerary enters, including countries where you only dock. Check each country against its own official travel page before you pay the balance.

Onboard medical care is typically not in the cruise fare. The CDC Yellow Book says so for ship medical centers in general. A month multiplies the chance you will want that insurance question answered before you sail.

Work, laundry, and the cabin

If you need the internet for work, read the line’s current package and the speed-test guide. A month of video calls is a package decision, not a vibe. Laundry is the other constraint: find out whether the ship sells wash-and-fold, whether there is a self-service laundry, and what it costs on your ship. Packing 30 outfits is how bags get refused at the airport.

Cabin location matters more on a long voyage because you sleep there more nights. Use the deck plan. A guarantee cabin that is fine for three nights can sit over a nightclub for a month.

Before you pay the deposit

Read the fare rules for name changes and for cancelling one segment of a back-to-back booking. A month-long plan often has a larger balance due than a week, and the penalty dates are in that fare’s terms, not in a general “cruises are flexible” claim. Ask the line what happens to the second cruise if you miss the first.

Count sea days and port days on the itinerary PDF. A month with almost no port days is a different trip from a month of overnight calls. If you work, the sea days are when the ship’s internet is the only connection. If you wanted cities, the sea days are the part you cannot swap later.

Share the cabin question early. A guarantee cabin can be assigned late. On a short cruise that is an annoyance. On a month it is who you live with. If you booked a specific cabin number, save the deck plan and the confirmation that the number survived any segment change.

What this page will not tell you

It will not tell you how a specific person felt, what they spent, or which ports were “worth it.” Those are folio and diary facts this site does not have. The line’s itinerary, the visa rules, the medical cover, and the internet package are the facts that decide whether a 30-day booking is workable.
