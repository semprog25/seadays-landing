How to choose a cruise cabin location

The best cabin location is the one that matches how you sleep and where you will actually walk. Category names — inside, ocean view, balcony, suite — describe the room. They do not describe the deck above you, the elevator bank beside you, or how far you are from the gangway on embarkation day.

What to check before you book

  • What is directly above and below the cabin: pool decks, theaters, and galleys are louder than a deck of other cabins.
  • How close you are to the elevators. Near is convenient and noisier. Far is quieter and a longer walk with bags.
  • Where you are along the length of the ship. Forward and aft feel more motion. Midship is usually calmer.
  • Whether the cabin connects to another room, if you are traveling with family who need that door.

Deck plans show these relationships better than a price grid. If the plan is vague, ask what is above the cabin before you treat a low fare as a bargain.

Location versus category

A cheaper category in a quiet midship spot can be a better trip than a balcony over a late-night venue. A balcony is worth it when you will use the outside space and you are comfortable with the deck it sits on. An inside cabin is a reasonable way to spend less when you are out of the room all day and you have checked that the cabin is not beside a noisy door or a crew area.

If you are sensitive to motion, favor midship and lower passenger decks over a high forward cabin with a better view. If you are sensitive to noise, favor a cabin with other cabins above and below it, away from the pool and the theater.

A simple rule

Pick the location first, then the category you can afford in that location. Write down the deck, the nearest elevator, and what the deck plan shows above the room. Those three facts prevent most of the “we should have checked” conversations after embarkation.
