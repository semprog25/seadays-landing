Overview

Celebrity Solstice was the first of Celebrity Cruises' five Solstice-class ships when it entered service in 2008. In March 2026 it came out of dry dock as the first ship in Celebrity's Solstice Series revitalization, with a new top deck and four new venues.

At 121,878 gross tons and 317 metres (1,041 ft) long, the ship carries 2,852 guests at double occupancy. That makes it mid-sized next to the largest resort ships at sea. Celebrity sells it as a modern premium ship: the focus is on restaurants, verandas and calmer evenings rather than rides.

What changed in 2026

Celebrity relaunched the ship from Singapore on 2 March 2026. The changes most guests will notice:

  • Sunset Park replaces the Lawn Club on the top deck. The real grass is now artificial turf, with lawn games, yoga, live music, private cabanas, the new Sunset Park Café for breakfast and lunch, and a larger Sunset Bar.
  • Trattoria Rossa is a new Italian restaurant serving Roman and Southern Italian dishes. Fine Cut Steakhouse was also added.
  • Boulevard Lounge is a new 125-seat venue for piano sing-alongs, karaoke and game shows.
  • The Parlor is a new sports and games lounge.
  • Every stateroom was refreshed, and 54 were added, for 1,479 in total. Celebrity also added a Retreat sundeck for suite guests and refreshed AquaClass.

Before the refit, Celebrity told Travel Weekly that the lawn games, movie screenings and concerts in Sunset Park would be free, while cabanas would cost extra. The glass-blowing studio is gone. If a cabana or a specialty restaurant matters to you, check the current price and what your fare includes before you sail.

Who Solstice suits

Couples and adults who want good food, a veranda and a quieter pool deck will like it most. There is a kids' program, Camp at Sea, so families are welcome. Families with teens who want waterslides and thrill rides will find more on the biggest resort ships.

Where it sails

In summer 2026 Celebrity Solstice sails 7-night round trips from Vancouver to Alaska, alternating between two routes from May to September:

  • Hubbard Glacier week: Icy Strait Point, Hubbard Glacier, Juneau and Ketchikan.
  • Dawes Glacier week: Icy Strait Point, Skagway, Endicott Arm and Dawes Glacier, Juneau and Ketchikan.

Want Skagway? Pick a Dawes Glacier week. From 13 September 2026 the ship sets off on a 110-night Grand Voyage to more than 55 ports in Alaska, the South Pacific, Australia, New Zealand and Asia, arriving in Hong Kong on New Year's Eve. Itineraries change, so confirm dates and ports with the cruise line.

Before you book

  • On Alaska glacier days, a veranda cabin lets you watch the scenery without fighting for rail space.
  • Sunset Park cabanas are a paid add-on, so ask about booking one early if you want it for a glacier or sea day.
  • The ship was rebuilt inside in 2026, so reviews from before March 2026 describe a different top deck.
